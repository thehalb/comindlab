**Abstract**

Reducing diagnostic error and understanding how
to prevent it remain critical goals of medical research. While
diagnostic error has been studied through a variety of approaches, recent scholarship has increasingly highlighted the
value of socialscience perspectives, particularly ethnographic
methods. Chopra (2020) introduced “focused ethnography” as
a valuable and efficient method for studying diagnostic errors
in healthcare settings. We extend this conversation by arguing
that more traditional ethnographic practices – especially
open-ended and iterative research design, extended fieldwork,
and researcher immersion in clinical communities – remain
essential for examining the complexity of medical diagnosis.
Diagnosis is now widely understood as a team-based process
involving physicians, nurses, patients and families, and other
healthcare professionals who exchange information and
distribute tasks to reach a diagnosis (Graber 2017). We argue
that only deep immersion in thisfundamentally social process
allows researchers to identify the subtle barriers and facilitators shaping diagnostic work and assess how diagnosis unfoldsin practice asteamwork. In this article, we illustrate how
classic ethnographic practices can be implemented to gain a
deeper understanding of diagnostic processes and highlight
some key methodological considerations when planning an
ethnography in a healthcare setting.
